Put the Kage 3D scene in the hero - the concept, not just the palette

Arkan: "MANA KONSEP KAGE NYA seperti 3d nya, ini mah original ke tampilan awal".
He was right. The previous pass took only the surface - font, colour, corners -
and the actual experience, the WebGL world, stayed quarantined on /kage. So the
scene is now the hero of the homepage.

How it is composed:

  .hero--kage       full-height hero. The scene sits behind everything as an
                    absolutely positioned layer; the portfolio's own headline
                    and copy sit on top at z-index 1.
  scrim             a dark gradient over the scene. Needed for legibility, not
                    decoration: the scene is textured and moving, so white text
                    on it has no stable contrast. Its colour is the same page
                    black, so it disappears.
  local tokens      the hero's text tokens are pinned to their light values,
                    because this hero always sits on dark media. In the light
                    theme the rest of the site is dark-on-white; the hero is
                    not, and would otherwise be unreadable.
  photo             moved out of the hero (it lost the fight with the scene)
                    to the quote section, where it closes the page as photo +
                    name + handle.
  duplicate         the "Scene" block further down the homepage is gone. The
                    scene is the hero now; pointing at it again would be a
                    second hero for the same thing.

The real bug, and it took three attempts to find:

The scene swallowed the scroll. Measured: wheel over the hero left
window.scrollY at 0 - a visitor could not leave the hero at all. The reason is
that the Kage document scrolls ITSELF (five chapters, body overflow hidden auto),
and wheel events reach it regardless of pointer-events.

  attempt 1  pointer-events: none on the wrapper. Not enough - wheel events
             still get delivered to the frame.
  attempt 2  a transparent guard layer over the scene. Still not enough: the
             measurement showed the wheel moving the INNER document.
  attempt 3  a client component that catches wheel/touch on the guard and
             redirects the delta to window.scrollBy. This is what works.

Wheel proved out at scrollY 1200 after a wheel gesture, where it was 0 before.

Then the touch path needed the same treatment, and the first two TOUCH TESTS I
wrote were wrong instruments rather than wrong code: mouse.down/move/up emits
mouse events (dragging a mouse does not scroll a page), and
Input.synthesizeScrollGesture never fired touchmove under emulation. A staged
Input.dispatchTouchEvent does move the page - and with it, the actual defect
appeared: touch-action was auto, so Chrome held the gesture and the handler was
never called (measured touchstart 1, touchmove 0). touch-action: pan-y
pinch-zoom fixed it; the swipe now scrolls 290px and touchmove fires 6 times.
Zoom is preserved.

40 gates green, including the two scroll traps and one that keeps the Kage
document from inflating the homepage height (6652px, not 13000).
